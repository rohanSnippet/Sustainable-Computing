Global Infrastructure Context

The Hyperscale Decarbonization Challenge

Why static 24/7 compute scheduling emits excessive carbon and inflates peak power costs across modern electricity grids.

1. Compute Growth Explosion >6x Growth
Global datacenters consume ~1% of worldwide electricity. Between 2010 and 2018, compute workloads expanded more than 6-fold. By 2030, ICT demand is projected to reach 3% to 13% of all global electricity, compounding climate impact unless managed dynamically.
2. Grid Carbon Volatility Hourly Swings
Greenhouse gas emissions from electricity vary dramatically by hour and region. Solar peaks produce abundant clean power midday, but evenings rely on carbon-intensive peaker plants. Running hyperscale datacenters flat burns dirty power unnecessarily.
3. Peak Provisioning Cost €1B/Year Grid Value
Datacenter power infrastructure (substations, bus ducts, generators) is provisioned for peak consumption ($/MW/day). Flattening peaks saves billions in capital infrastructure while unlocking up to €1B/year in value for electric grid operators.
The Paradigm Shift: From Passive Load to Dynamic Grid Asset Google Core Thesis

Instead of treating datacenters as rigid power consumers that follow unpredictable user demand blindly, hyperscale compute can be molded as flexible demand that actively consumes power when and where local grid energy is cleanest.

Cluster Operating System Integration

Virtual Capacity Curves (VCCs)

Decoupling carbon optimization from real-time scheduling: Borg processes hundreds of thousands of jobs/sec without carbon overhead.

How Borg Executes VCC Two-Step Optimization
  1. Step 1 (CICS Optimizer): Runs at 6:00 PM PT. Generates 24 hourly VCC limits per cluster across Google's fleet.
  2. Step 2 (Borg Scheduler): Borg views VCC as its physical CPU capacity ceiling. When VCC dips during dirty hours, admission control holds incoming batch jobs in queue.
VCC(c)(h) = (ÛIF + (1 + δ) τU/24) R̂(c)(h)

Zero modifications to Borg scheduling algorithms. Completely modular and resilient against scheduler crashes.

Google Site Reliability Engineering (SRE)

Production Safety & The 97th Percentile Rule

How CICS guarantees zero impact on user SLAs: robust statistical safety buffers and automatic 7-day circuit breakers.

The 97th Percentile Safety Buffer ≤ 3% Risk Bound
Flexible daily compute is a stochastic process. To ensure jobs are never starved, the optimizer plans against the 97th percentile of total daily reservations:
Θ(c)(d) = T̂R(c)(d) [1 + ({ε(c)(n)}).97]
Evaluated over rolling 90-day historical forecast errors. Extra buffer is injected by inflating flexible budget with factor α(c)(d) ≥ 1.
Strict Workload SLO Target ≤ 1 Day / Month
The strict operational objective states: The cluster-level daily flexible compute capacity requirement cannot be violated more than 1 day per month (roughly 3 days in a 100-day window).

If actual demand exceeds the 97th percentile threshold for 2 consecutive days, the cluster enters emergency protection mode.

Automatic 7-Day Fail-Safe Trip Autonomous Guard
When the 2-day trigger fires, CICS completely disables load shaping for that cluster for 7 consecutive days.

This gives the load forecasting ML models a full week to adapt to structural traffic growth or sudden transient spikes before re-engaging carbon optimization.

Unbiased User Delay Guarantee Impartial Queuing

When flexible compute is queued during high-carbon hours, Borg's admission controller delays incoming jobs in an unbiased manner across internal engineering teams. No single application team bears unfair latency penalties, ensuring organization-wide buy-in.

Cluster Y Analysis 3-Hour Window
In clusters with higher workload variance:
  • The 97th percentile risk buffer forces the average VCC value ~33% higher than actual demand.
  • VCC still drops ~50%, but the window is narrower: 3 hours vs 6 hours.

Controlled experiment conducted across entire Google campus:

Long-Term Decarbonization Vision

Conclusion & The Next Frontier: Spatial Shifting

Transforming the world's largest compute infrastructure from a static energy burden into a dynamic, planet-scale climate asset.

1. Proven Production Feasibility First at Scale
Google CICS demonstrates that carbon-aware computing can be deployed at hyperscale across 20+ datacenters and 15.5 TWh of electricity with zero disruption to real-time production Borg scheduling or end-user cloud availability.
2. Spatial Compute Shifting Follow the Sun & Wind
While CICS shifts batch jobs in time within a cluster, the ongoing expansion shifts compute across geographies. When night falls in Europe, flexible workloads migrate across transatlantic fiber to US solar grids or Nordic wind farms.
3. Path to 24/7 Carbon-Free Energy Google 2030 Goal
Temporal and spatial compute shaping provides the software foundation for Google's ambitious pledge to operate on 100% 24/7 Carbon-Free Energy (CFE) by 2030, co-optimizing with wholesale energy markets.
